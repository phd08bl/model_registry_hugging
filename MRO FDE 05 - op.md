The most efficient arrangement is to maintain **one FDE case record for each prioritised process**. The cards guide conversations, the checklists test completeness, and the templates capture the agreed outputs.

Do not ask stakeholders to complete all cards and 26 questions themselves. The FDE lead should facilitate the discussions, review evidence and populate the case record.

# Stage 1: Candidate process problem and outcome

## Purpose

Capture enough information for the FDE team to understand the candidate and prepare it for leadership prioritisation. This is not detailed discovery and does not approve a tool.

The sponsoring IVT head or Grade G colleague, with light support from the FDE team.

## Stage 1 checklist

- [ ] The candidate describes a process problem rather than only requesting a tool.
- [ ] A recent example has been provided.
- [ ] The affected users and teams are identified.

# Proportionate use of the toolkit

| Case route | Required materials |
|---|---|
| Light | Candidate Card, Leadership Decision, relevant parts of Cards 2/4/7, short action plan and process-owner acceptance |
| Standard | All relevant discovery cards, options assessment, experiment charter, evaluation record, delivery/handover and benefits review |
| Enhanced | Full toolkit, architecture and data review, formal methodology control, independent challenge, controlled rollout and detailed monitoring |

The practical discipline is:

> **Use cards to ask, evidence to verify, templates to record, checklists to challenge and gates to decide.**

This keeps the FDE process evidence-led without turning it into a documentation exercise.



The current MRO Tech & Tooling developer squad is the **technical discovery, experiment and delivery capability** within the FDE process. Its main work sits in Stages 4, 5 and 7, with lighter involvement in Stages 3, 6 and 8.

The squad should not own leadership prioritisation or define the business process. The relevant process owner remains accountable for the outcome, requirements, controls and acceptance.

## Squad involvement by stage

| FDE stage | Developer squad involvement | Expected squad output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Candidate process problem | Normally none; clarify an existing tool or backlog item if needed | Technical context only |
| 2. Leadership priority | Normally none; provide indicative capacity or dependency information if requested | High-level feasibility/capacity note |
| 3. FDE discovery | A technical representative attends relevant systems, data and workflow discussions | Constraints, existing capability and technical questions |
| 4. Options and delivery route | Technical lead helps assess feasibility, architecture, reuse and delivery ownership | Technical options and recommended route |
| 5. Vertical slice or experiment | Squad builds or configures the smallest credible experiment | Prototype, test evidence and technical findings |
| 6. Evaluation and decision | Squad explains results, failures, limitations and production implications | Technical evaluation and delivery estimate |
| 7. Delivery and rollout | Squad develops, tests, documents and hands over where it is the agreed delivery owner | Released capability and handover pack |
| 8. Adoption, benefits and reuse | Squad resolves technical defects and extracts reusable patterns | Improvements, reusable components and technical lifecycle plan |

## Stages 1 and 2: Limited involvement

The developer squad should not begin designing or building when a candidate is submitted.

At these stages, the questions are:

- Does the process problem matter?
- What outcome needs to improve?
- Who owns the process?
- Should leadership allocate discovery capacity?

The squad may provide a brief factual response where an existing tool, platform dependency or ongoing initiative is relevant. It should not estimate a full build from a short tool request because the process and requirements have not yet been established.

Leadership approval at Stage 2 means:

> “Investigate this process problem through FDE.”

It does not mean:

> “The MRO developer squad has committed to build this tool.”

## Stage 3: Technical discovery support

The FDE case lead conducts the process discovery. One nominated member of the developer squad should join the parts covering:

- Current systems and tools.
- Data and evidence sources.
- Access and permissions.
- Existing components.
- Technical constraints.
- Known integration options.
- Production feasibility.

## Stage 4: Technical options and route assessment

This is the first stage where the squad has a substantial design role.

The FDE lead and process owner define what the intervention must achieve. The technical lead then helps compare:

- Process or guidance change.
- Existing MRO capability.
- Enterprise platform capability.
- Copilot prompt or agent.
- Power Automate or deterministic workflow.
- Python analytical service.
- Cortex agent and MRO harness.
- Combined solution.

The squad should answer:

- Can an existing component be reused?
- What is the smallest viable intervention?
- Does the solution require code?
- Does it require specialist MRO methodology?
- Can Copilot or Power Platform support it?
- Is an MRO analytical component needed?
- Does it require the Cortex agent harness?
- What production route is possible?
- Which team is best placed to deliver and support it?
- What should be tested before delivery is committed?

The result may determine that another platform team should deliver the solution. In that case, the MRO squad can still provide methodology, acceptance criteria and technical assurance without becoming the primary builder.

## Stage 5: Developer squad builds the experiment

This is normally the squad’s first hands-on development stage.

A small case-specific squad is formed from the current contributor pool. It could include:

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sprint owner | Coordinates the experiment and removes blockers |
| FDE case lead | Protects the outcome, scope and experiment question |
| Technical lead | Chooses the prototype design and technical pattern |
| Builder(s) | Develop or configure the vertical slice |
| Process SME | Explains the process and methodology |
| User/tester | Runs representative cases |
| Quality/evaluation lead | Checks evidence against acceptance criteria |

The sprint should begin from an approved experiment charter. The squad should not receive a general instruction such as “build a report-writing agent.”

A suitable sprint item would be:

> Using five completed validation cases and user-selected evidence, test whether a controlled drafting assistant can produce evidence-linked report sections while reducing total drafting and correction time.

### Experiment backlog

The Jira epic can contain:

1. Technical spike.
2. Data and access preparation.
3. Minimum workflow.
4. Tool or agent configuration.
5. Representative test cases.
6. Evidence capture.
7. Failure and exception testing.
8. User review.
9. Technical findings.
10. Demonstration and evaluation pack.

The output of this stage is evidence, not a production commitment.

## Stage 6: Squad supports the decision

The developer squad should demonstrate:

- What was built.
- What worked.
- What failed.
- Technical limitations.
- Integration assumptions.
- Security and access constraints.
- Expected production changes.
- Reusable components.
- Indicative delivery and support needs.

The process owner and users assess business suitability. The FDE or quality lead assesses whether the experiment answered the agreed question. The proposed service owner considers operational viability.

The squad should not mark its own demonstration as production-ready without these other decisions.

## Stage 7: Delivery through the existing sprint model

If AI Tech & Tooling is the agreed delivery owner, the approved scope enters the existing sprint process.

The current squad structure can remain largely unchanged:

- Sprint planning.
- Jira stories.
- Builders.
- SMEs.
- Testers.
- Sprint demonstrations.
- Retrospectives.
- Documentation in Confluence.

The main change is that new delivery epics arrive with:

- A leadership-prioritised outcome.
- A named process owner.
- Discovery evidence.
- An agreed delivery route.
- Acceptance criteria.
- A tested experiment.
- Clear ownership and support expectations.

This should reduce unclear tool requests and repeated changes during development.

### Squad Definition of Ready

Before the squad accepts a delivery item:

- [ ] Process owner is named.
- [ ] Desired outcome is agreed.
- [ ] User group is identified.
- [ ] Scope and exclusions are clear.
- [ ] Acceptance criteria are testable.
- [ ] Process and control requirements are recorded.
- [ ] Data and access are available.
- [ ] Experiment evidence supports the route.
- [ ] Architecture and hosting route are understood.
- [ ] Operational owner is proposed.
- [ ] Required SMEs and testers are committed.
- [ ] Delivery capacity has been approved.

## Stage 8: Technical support and reuse

After release, the process owner owns adoption and benefits. The squad contributes by:

- Monitoring technical performance.
- Resolving defects.
- Supporting early rollout.
- Reviewing agent or tool failures.
- Maintaining supported components.
- Implementing approved changes.
- Identifying reusable patterns.

Reusable assets could include:

- Document ingestion components.
- Evidence and citation schemas.
- Human approval patterns.
- Agent tool interfaces.
- Audit and tracing components.
- Prompt templates.
- Evaluation datasets.
- Query-log processing modules.
- Report-generation components.
- Envoy-ready deployment patterns.

These should enter a shared component backlog rather than being copied separately into each new tool.

## How current work should transition

Existing work should not be stopped and restarted. Apply a proportionate FDE gap review.

### Tools already in development

For each active tool, confirm:

- What process and outcome it supports.
- Who the process owner is.
- Who the users are.
- What acceptance criteria apply.
- What evidence the current prototype provides.
- Which delivery and operational route is expected.
- Who will support it after the current contributors finish.

Continue the current sprint if these points are sufficiently clear. Add missing items to the existing epic.

### Existing demonstrations and prototypes

Treat these as Stage 5 experiments. Assess what they have actually demonstrated and what remains untested before moving them into delivery.

### Existing idea backlog

Map each idea to:

- Underlying process problem.
- Desired outcome.
- Sponsor.
- Proposed process owner.
- Related FDE candidate.

Ideas without an identified process problem or owner remain in the candidate backlog rather than entering a development sprint.

Joe and Sai can both lead individual FDE cases. A case lead does not have to be the principal developer. This allows one person to protect the process outcome while another leads implementation.

## Backlog structure

Keep the current Jira setup, but separate the work into five views:

1. **Candidate process backlog**  
   Submitted process problems awaiting leadership consideration.

2. **Active FDE discovery**  
   Prioritised cases being understood and assessed.

3. **Technical experiments**  
   Approved vertical slices and technical spikes.

4. **Delivery backlog**  
   Solutions approved for implementation.

5. **Reusable capability backlog**  
   Components, templates and patterns that support several cases.

Each item should retain the same FDE case ID across all views so leadership can trace a process problem through discovery, experiment, delivery and benefits.

The developer squad therefore remains central, but it receives better-shaped work. Its purpose within FDE is to **test technical assumptions, build the agreed solution and create reusable capability**, while leadership and process owners retain the decisions about priority, process, methodology and acceptance.
